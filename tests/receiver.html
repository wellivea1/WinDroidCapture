WinDroid Capture — local receiver test

This page stays on this PC. It makes no calls, sends no media to a server, records nothing, and does not play the microphone through speakers. Device discovery, OBS output, and receiving usable phone video/audio are separate tests.

Choose both endpoints explicitly

First use: this page does not open unidentified/default inputs just to trigger a browser permission prompt. If labels are withheld, manually allow camera and microphone for this localhost page through browser site controls and refresh. If your browser cannot pregrant those permissions, use the supplied native C receiver instead; it selects the exact Windows endpoints directly. This page refuses default, unselected, or non-OBS cameras.

Native receiver command (no browser permission prompt)
awm-receiver.exe --camera "OBS Virtual Camera" --microphone "CABLE Output (VB-Audio Virtual Cable)" --duration 180

Run from the published package, or use build/awm-receiver.exe from the source workspace. Substitute your exact cable recording name. The helper displays local video and a meter, records nothing, and does not play through speakers.

Receiver NOT RUN. Select both intended devices.
Physical verification (requires a person at the phone)
  1. Point the selected phone camera at a distinctive object and move it. Confirm it appears here, not the PC's webcam.
  2. Speak a unique phrase into the phone and lightly tap near its microphone. The cable meter must follow the phone pattern; silence other apps using the cable.
  3. Use a visible clap/tap for synchronization. Watch the hands meet and the meter transient. Record observations manually; this page does not claim a latency measurement.
  4. Test output rotation and mirroring by showing written text and an asymmetric object. This page applies no additional mirror transform.
  5. Press Stop in the manager while leaving this receiver running. Confirm the phone image and signal cease. Test unplug/reconnect and a sustained session.

The reported receiver dimensions are its negotiated format. An upstream 720p OBS output can be rescaled into a larger receiver format; that does not establish genuine 1080p/4K detail.
